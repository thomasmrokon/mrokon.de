About me

Thomas Mrokon is Professor of Design and Digital Construction at Mainz University of Applied Sciences and, since 2024, Program Director of Architecture.

With the award-winning buildings “House of Astronomy, Heidelberg” and “ESO Supernova, Garching”, he was able to combine his expertise in computational design and digital fabrication with practical innovative strength.

After an entrepreneurial phase with his own startup (monomer), he joined the new architecture department at Drees&Sommer until he was appointed to Mainz University of Applied Sciences in 2021.

Alongside a focus on immersive spatial teaching with VR/AR tools, since 2023 his research has increasingly centered on AI applications in the construction industry, which are now taught in a dedicated course module.

His focus on digital tools lies in their transformative impact across the entire value chain. This results in both individual and societal implications for all areas of life.

Since 2023, Thomas Mrokon has been giving regular keynotes and talks on AI for diverse audiences.

My vision: A world in which technology is not only used for efficiency gains or perceived as a threat, but as a means of preserving freedom, fostering personal growth, and creating meaning.

Focus Areas

Digital Design

Parametric design and algorithmic architecture enable complex, data-driven design processes. Computational design has raised planning complexity and quality to a very high level. Multi-criteria analyses and optioneering have become technical methods that bring sustainability, economic factors, and architectural quality into responsible balance.

Immersive Spatial Teaching with VR/AR

Immersion through VR/AR expands spatial design processes by adding the important third dimension. This creates new freedoms beyond two-dimensional abstraction.

Disruptions in the Construction Industry

The slow-moving construction industry is undergoing massive change through AI across all phases and the entire value chain. While BIM was optional, AI is becoming essential.

AI as a Transformative Technology

AI combined with robotics has massive social, societal, and geopolitical impacts on our work, our environment, and our shared future. The ethical dimensions of hyperautomation, efficiency strategies, and digital species demand broad discourse across society, business, and politics.

ESO Supernova, Garching

The ESO Supernova Visitor Center interprets a binary star system just before supernova – two dancing cores wrapped in a breaking shell, with a planetarium in one and a glass dome in the other, linked by ramps and gallery levels. Fully modeled parametrically and completed in 2019.

House of Astronomy, Heidelberg

Architecturally conceived as a parametrically generated spiral galaxy, the nearly column-free House of Astronomy rests on only eight pillars, two cores, and a segmented concrete dome. Its complex façade of 392 spherical, multi-folded metal components without a single parallel edge offers a monumental interpretation of space.

raumsyntax

A research project with congena GmbH shows how agent-based simulations can analyze movement flows in buildings and neighborhoods during early design, enabling data-driven optimization of circulation, quality of stay, and functional efficiency. Further projects with BMW Group for IAA 2003 and 2005 followed.

→ Project Report

Diploma Thesis

For my 2004 diploma thesis, an Amsterdam district was analyzed using agent-based simulation: digital “residents” with varying daily routines (work, shopping, leisure, etc.) moved through a virtual model while their paths, times, and stays were recorded. Structural changes – e.g., new bridges or block divisions – could thus be quantitatively assessed. Four urban design concepts were compared for efficiency and spatial effect. The method demonstrated how architects can simulate user behavior, critically test designs, and steer urban development processes participatively – essentially my first experience with artificial intelligence.

Teaching

Informed Architecture

Students learn to use digital tools to critically evaluate designs in the early stages and make the best possible decisions. Alongside Autodesk Forma, CAALA and ecotool are used to integrate lifecycle assessment as a key aspect of design from the beginning.

Immersive Spatial Teaching

The design process in virtual space is a novel experience for many students. New geometric freedoms and direct spatial perception through physical interaction make the design process tangible. VR/AR tools such as Arkio and GravitySketch closely merge virtuality and reality.

New Business Models with AI

In the module "Digital Construction Processes," students gain insights into current AI developments in the construction industry and beyond. Individually or in teams, they develop innovative disruptive business models that challenge established value chains.
